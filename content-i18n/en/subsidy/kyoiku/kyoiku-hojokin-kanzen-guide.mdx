Japan's education cost support has three main parts: the "High School Tuition Support Fund (高等学校等就学支援金)" for high school students, "grant-type scholarships (給付型奨学金)" for university students and others, and "school attendance assistance (就学援助)" for elementary and junior high school students. The high school and university programs use nationwide criteria, but school attendance assistance has income criteria and covered items that differ by city or ward.

## Key points

- The **High School Tuition Support Fund** is scheduled to have its **income limit removed** from fiscal 2026 (Reiwa 8), based on an agreement among three political parties
- The payment cap is **¥118,800 per year** for public schools and **¥457,200 per year** for private full-time schools
- **Grant-type scholarships** (JASSO) for university and similar schools have a monthly amount set by household income bracket (Brackets 1 to 4)
- **School attendance assistance** (for elementary and junior high students) has income criteria and covered items that differ by municipality

## 1. High School Tuition Support Fund: income limit removed from fiscal 2026

This nationwide program pays an amount equivalent to a high school student's tuition. According to the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) budget materials for fiscal 2026 (Reiwa 8), based on the agreement among the Liberal Democratic Party, Komeito, and Nippon Ishin no Kai (October 29 and December 18, 2025), the **income limit is fully removed from the fiscal 2026 (Reiwa 8) budget**.

<Callout type="warning">
Through fiscal 2025 (Reiwa 7), the program had household income criteria of under ¥5,900,000 and under ¥9,100,000 in annual income. The new system from fiscal 2026 removes this income limit. However, as a transitional measure, the old criteria may still apply to some current students, so please check with your school or prefecture to find out which system applies to you.
</Callout>

### Payment cap (per year)

| School type | Cap |
|---|---|
| Public high school | ¥118,800 |
| Private high school (full-time) | ¥457,200 |
| Private high school (correspondence) | ¥337,200 |
| National high school | ¥115,200 |

The amount is only a cap. If tuition is lower, the actual tuition amount is paid. The national government bears three quarters of the cost and the prefecture one quarter (public and private schools); for national schools, the national government bears all of it.

## 2. Grant-type scholarships (university, junior college, technical college, vocational school): JASSO

Under the "New Support System for Higher Education (高等教育の修学支援新制度)" run by the Japan Student Services Organization (JASSO, 日本学生支援機構), a scholarship that does not need to be repaid is paid according to household income. It can be used together with tuition and admission fee reductions.

### Income brackets (guideline for on-enrollment selection, two-person household of salaried workers)

| Bracket | Guideline household income |
|---|---|
| Bracket 1 | ¥2,290,000 |
| Bracket 2 | ¥3,320,000 |
| Bracket 3 | ¥4,020,000 |
| Bracket 4 (multi-child households only) | ¥6,490,000 |

The guideline amounts change depending on household composition. The monthly amount also differs depending on whether you live at home or away from home, and whether the school is national/public or private.

### Monthly amount example (national/public school, living at home)

| Bracket | University, junior college, vocational school |
|---|---|
| Bracket 1 | ¥29,200 |
| Bracket 2 | ¥19,500 |
| Bracket 3 | ¥9,800 |
| Bracket 4 | ¥7,300 |

The amount is higher for students living away from home or attending private schools (example: Bracket 1 for a private school student living away from home is ¥75,800 per month). As an asset criterion, the combined assets of the student and the two persons supporting their livelihood must also be under ¥50,000,000.

## 3. School attendance assistance (elementary and junior high students, by city/ward)

This program helps parents of children attending elementary and junior high schools (including compulsory education schools and the first half of secondary education schools) pay for school supplies, school lunches, school trips, and similar costs. It covers households on public assistance, and households that the board of education recognizes as being in similar circumstances (quasi-eligible households, 準要保護).

Income criteria and covered items differ by city or ward. Specific amounts are explained in each municipality's article.
