The Newlywed Support Program (結婚新生活支援事業) is a subsidy in which municipalities cover part of the housing purchase costs, rent, moving costs and similar expenses of newlywed households. The national government (Children and Families Agency) pays part of the funding through a grant, but **whether to run the program is up to each municipality**, so it is not available uniformly across Japan.

<SubsidyInfoCard />

## Key takeaways

- The Newlywed Support Program is **run at each municipality's discretion**. It is a grant system in which the national government covers part of the funding; the national government does not pay individuals directly
- The national grant requirements (standards) are up to ¥600,000 if both spouses are 29 or younger, and up to ¥300,000 otherwise (39 or younger)
- **None of Tokyo's 23 special wards was running it as of May 29, 2025** (confirmed in national published materials). Only some municipalities, such as Tachikawa and Ome, were

## 1. How the program works: a national grant, with municipalities deciding whether to participate

The Newlywed Support Program is one of the menu items under the Children and Families Agency's "Regional Measures for Declining Birthrate Priority Promotion Grant" (地域少子化対策重点推進交付金). When a municipality runs this program, the national government covers part of the cost through the grant.

<Callout type="warning">
The important point is that this program is not available uniformly nationwide. Unless the municipality decides to run it, its residents cannot use it. Whether it is offered, the amount and the conditions must be checked with each municipality.
</Callout>

## 2. National grant requirements (standards)

The standards the national government uses when providing the grant are as follows. The amount and conditions residents actually receive may differ depending on how the participating municipality designs its program.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Eligible households | Newlywed households where both spouses are 39 or younger, with household income under ¥5 million (for households repaying student loans, the repayment amount is deducted from income) |
| Eligible costs | Purchase of a new home; rent, security deposit, key money, common service fees and agent fees; renovation costs; moving costs |
| Maximum subsidy (both spouses 29 or younger) | Up to ¥600,000 per household (¥300,000 national share) |
| Maximum subsidy (others, 39 or younger) | Up to ¥300,000 per household (¥150,000 national share) |

## 3. Implementation status: only Tachikawa and Ome confirmed in Tokyo

According to the usage-status materials published by the Children and Families Agency as of May 29, 2025, only two cities in Tokyo were confirmed to be running the Newlywed Support Program (cash subsidy type): **Tachikawa** and **Ome**. No implementation was confirmed for the 23 special wards or any other municipality in Tokyo in the materials as of that date.

<Callout type="info">
This reflects the fact that "two cities had applied to the national government for the grant by May 29, 2025." It does not mean that "the 23 wards are unable to run it under the system." It is possible that some municipalities have started since then. Please check your own municipality's official website to see whether it is running the program.
</Callout>

National materials also confirm that in neighboring Saitama Prefecture, several cities and towns, including Kawaguchi, run this program. If you are looking for newlywed support near a large city in Tokyo, it can also help to look across prefectural borders.

## 4. How it differs from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's own marriage support

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government runs its own programs such as "TOKYO Futari STORY" (TOKYOふたりSTORY). Unlike the Newlywed Support Program (cash subsidy), these focus on creating opportunities to meet people and building momentum for marriage, through AI matching, matchmaking events and point campaigns. If you want a cash subsidy for housing and moving costs, you need to find a municipality that runs the Newlywed Support Program.
