The long-term care insurance home renovation benefit lets people certified as needing support or long-term care get help from long-term care insurance for home renovations such as installing handrails or removing level differences — up to ¥200,000 over a lifetime. This is a nationwide program, and you apply through your local municipality (the insurer).

<SubsidyInfoCard />

## Key points

- The eligible spending cap is **¥200,000 over a lifetime** (a flat amount regardless of your care-need level)
- Insurance generally covers **90%** (less for higher-income households)
- Apply to your municipality **before the work begins**, in principle

## 1. Who qualifies

You qualify if you're certified as needing support or long-term care. Eligible work is limited to these six categories:

- Installing handrails
- Removing level differences
- Changing flooring material to prevent slipping or ease movement
- Replacing doors with sliding doors, etc.
- Replacing toilets with Western-style ones, etc.
- Other incidental work necessary alongside the above renovations

## 2. Amount and how it's calculated

The eligible spending cap is a flat ¥200,000 over a lifetime, regardless of your care-need level. The percentage covered by insurance varies by income.

| Income bracket | Coverage rate | Maximum benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | 90% | ¥180,000 |
| Higher-income | 80% | ¥160,000 |
| Higher-income (even higher) | 70% | ¥140,000 |

You can split your applications across multiple projects within the ¥200,000 cap. Also, if your care-need level increases by three or more grades, or if you move to a new home, a new ¥200,000 spending cap is set for you.

## 3. How to apply and required documents

Applications generally happen in two stages: before and after the work.

<Checklist items={["Consult with your care manager or similar", "Choose a contractor and get a quote", "Submit a pre-construction application to your municipality (including the benefit application form, construction cost quote, a statement explaining why the renovation is needed, and something showing what the finished result will look like)", "Have the work done once your municipality confirms it's approved", "After the work is complete, apply to your municipality for the benefit payment (including receipts, an itemized cost breakdown, and before/after photos)"]} />

In unavoidable circumstances, you can also apply after the work is finished, combining both stages into one submission.
