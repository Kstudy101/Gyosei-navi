To ease the burden of long-term care insurance co-payments and at-home caregiving, each of Tokyo's 23 wards runs its own supplementary assistance. But the details vary widely by ward. This article compares six: Chiyoda, Minato, Shinagawa, Setagaya, Shibuya, and Shinjuku — organized not by "which has the biggest amount" but by "what kind of support each one provides."

## Key points

- The six wards' programs split into four broad types: **in-kind supply** (Chiyoda, Shinjuku), **co-payment reduction** (Minato, Shibuya), **monitoring support** (Shinagawa), and **at-home care allowance** (Setagaya)
- While most programs assume a long-term care certification, **only Shinagawa's monitoring system doesn't require one**
- Minato and Shibuya both run similar "co-payment reduction" programs, but their **rate calculations** (7/10, or half of the amount over a threshold, versus a flat 70%) and **income/asset requirements** differ

## Why a simple amount comparison doesn't work

Like home renovation subsidies, ward-specific long-term care assistance addresses fundamentally different situations.

- Chiyoda and Shinjuku make it cheaper to buy a **physical item — diapers**
- Minato and Shibuya reduce **your actual co-payment percentage** for services used
- Shinagawa provides a **free emergency monitoring and dispatch service**
- Setagaya pays a **flat-amount allowance to families caring at home**

Lining these up by "which has the highest reduction rate" compares fundamentally different kinds of numbers, which can be misleading.

## Program type by ward

**① In-kind supply type (Chiyoda, Shinjuku)**

These reduce the cost of buying diapers. Chiyoda is for people certified as needing long-term care level 1 or higher: purchases up to ¥10,600 a month cost just 10%, and households on public assistance pay nothing (there's also a cash subsidy, up to ¥13,200 a month, for people who are hospitalized or in a facility). Shinjuku covers people at long-term care level 1 or higher too, at roughly a 10% co-payment for purchases up to ¥10,000 a month. Both wards work primarily through a catalog-order system, but Shinjuku also offers a separate "cash subsidy" route — based on submitting receipts — for people hospitalized at facilities that don't accept diaper deliveries.

**② Co-payment reduction type (Minato, Shibuya)**

These reduce part of your out-of-pocket cost when using long-term care insurance services. Minato subsidizes 7/10 of your co-payment for home-visit-type services, or half of whatever falls between ¥15,000 and ¥24,600 a month (up to ¥4,800/month). Shibuya subsidizes a flat 70% of your co-payment across a wide range of services, including home-visit care and day care. Both wards cap eligibility by household income and savings, and neither is available to households on public assistance.

**③ Monitoring support type (Shinagawa)**

This provides a free emergency response system for residents 65 or older living alone or in elderly-only households. Unlike the other five wards, **it doesn't require any long-term care certification**. If an emergency pendant or sensor is triggered, it notifies a security company, which dispatches help.

**④ At-home care allowance type (Setagaya)**

Families who care at home for a year — without much reliance on long-term care insurance services — for someone certified as needing long-term care level 2 (dementia independence level 2 or higher) or level 3 or above receive an annual allowance of ¥100,000. Unlike the other five wards, which assume "using services," this program requires "the family cared for the person without using services" — an interesting contrast.
