Every one of Tokyo's 23 wards also runs its own subsidy program in the energy and environment space. But the equipment and mechanisms covered vary widely by ward. This article compares six: Chiyoda, Minato, Shinagawa, Setagaya, Shibuya, and Shinjuku — organized not by "which has the biggest amount" but by "what kind of support each one provides."

## Key points

- Four wards include **ENE-FARM (home fuel cell systems)** as an eligible item — Minato, Shinagawa, Shibuya, and Shinjuku — but the calculation method (fixed amount vs. fixed rate) differs by ward
- Chiyoda covers **EV charging equipment and V2H**, Setagaya covers **switching your electricity plan**, and Shinjuku offers a **menu of six device types** — each fundamentally different from the rest
- **Setagaya alone isn't a program you apply for directly at the ward office**, and **Shinjuku alone requires you to apply after construction and payment are fully complete** — each structurally distinct from the other wards

## Why a simple amount comparison doesn't work

As with home renovation subsidies and long-term care assistance, ward-specific energy and environment programs differ in what they're subsidizing in the first place.

- Chiyoda covers **EV charging equipment and V2H installation costs**
- Minato, Shinagawa, Shibuya, and Shinjuku cover **ENE-FARM (home fuel cell system) installation costs** (Shinjuku also covers five other device types)
- Setagaya covers **switching your electricity plan** (not equipment investment)

Lining these up by "which has the highest subsidy" compares dollar amounts for fundamentally different kinds of support, which can be misleading.

## Program type by ward

**① EV and V2H installation type (Chiyoda)**

This subsidizes the cost of installing clean-energy vehicle charging equipment (fast or standard) and V2H. Fast chargers and V2H get up to ¥500,000, and standard chargers up to ¥300,000, but the combined cap if applying for multiple units is ¥500,000. It can be combined with Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry or Tokyo metropolitan subsidies.

**② ENE-FARM installation type (Minato, Shinagawa, Shibuya, Shinjuku)**

All four wards include installing a home fuel cell system (ENE-FARM) as an eligible item, but the calculation method differs. Shibuya offers a flat ¥300,000, Shinagawa up to ¥200,000 (though it stops accepting applications once it hits its quota of 30 units), Minato equipment cost divided by 4 up to ¥150,000, and Shinjuku a flat ¥100,000. Minato is the only one of the four that excludes installation labor from eligible costs.

**③ Electricity contract switching type (Setagaya)**

While the other wards target equipment purchases and installation, Setagaya's renewable energy switch subsidy targets residents switching their electricity plan to a 100% renewable plan. You don't apply directly at the ward office — instead, you sign up through a campaign run by a ward-approved electric company, and get back up to ¥15,000 per contract switch (household).

**④ Multi-device menu, apply-after-the-fact type (Shinjuku)**

This "menu-style" program lets you choose from six categories of eligible equipment: solar power, eco-friendly water heaters, ENE-FARM, high-reflectivity coating, insulated windows, and battery storage systems. A battery storage system gets ¥10,000 per kWh of capacity (up to ¥100,000), but it must always be connected to solar power or an ENE-FARM. Unlike most other wards, which require applying before construction, Shinjuku's program is structurally different: **you apply only after construction and payment are fully complete**. There are four application windows a year, and each closes once its budget cap is reached — even mid-window.
