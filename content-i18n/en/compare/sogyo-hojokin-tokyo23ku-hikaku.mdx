Each of Tokyo's 23 wards also runs its own program to encourage startups, but the mechanisms differ significantly by ward. This article compares six: Chiyoda, Minato, Shinagawa, Setagaya, Shibuya, and Shinjuku — organized around the fundamental question of "is this a loan you repay, or a subsidy you don't."

## Key points

- Only Minato offers a **cash subsidy you don't have to repay** (2/3 of eligible costs, up to ¥2,500,000); the other five wards **arrange low-interest loans** (which eventually need repaying)
- The five loan-brokerage wards significantly lower your self-funded cost by having the ward subsidize most of the interest — **Shibuya has the lowest at 0.1% or less**
- Shinagawa breaks its terms into finer categories — "first-time startup," "second business," and more — each with different rates and guarantee fee subsidy rates

## Why a simple amount comparison doesn't work

Like the long-term care and energy assistance programs, startup support differs in the fundamental "character of the money" involved.

- Minato offers **cash subsidies** (the ward pays out part of your eligible costs directly, no repayment needed)
- Chiyoda, Shinagawa, Setagaya, Shibuya, and Shinjuku all arrange **low-interest loans** (a three-way cooperation between the ward, a credit guarantee association, and a financial institution, with the ward subsidizing part of the interest; the principal still needs to be repaid)

Lining these up by "which has the highest cap" compares money you don't have to pay back against money you borrowed, which can be misleading.

## Program type by ward

**① Cash subsidy type (Minato)**

Businesses less than two years into their startup get up to ¥2,500,000 (2/3 of eligible costs) in cash, covering rent, equipment costs, PR costs, and website creation costs combined. Unlike a loan, there's no repayment required, but only about 100 slots are available, and applications close once the budget is used up.

**② Loan brokerage type (Chiyoda, Shinagawa, Setagaya, Shibuya, Shinjuku)**

These arrange low-interest loans through a three-way cooperation between the ward, a credit guarantee association, and a financial institution, with the ward subsidizing part of the interest. Loan limits are mostly around ¥20,000,000 (Chiyoda offers ¥25,000,000 for residents), but the self-funded cost (your share of the interest rate) varies by ward.

| Ward | Your cost | Guarantee fee subsidy |
|---|---|---|
| Chiyoda | 0.4% or less | Fully subsidized (exempt from the responsibility-sharing system) |
| Shinagawa (first-time startup) | 0.2% or less | Fully subsidized |
| Setagaya | 0.2% | Tokyo subsidy (2/3) may apply |
| Shibuya | 0.1% or less | Tokyo subsidy (2/3) may apply |
| Shinjuku | 0.2% or less | Half of the amount paid (up to ¥260,000) |

Shinagawa breaks its terms into categories like "first-time startup," "second business," and "Designated Startup Support Program special provision," with those certified under the Designated Startup Support Program getting zero interest through their third year. Shinjuku's loan limit is ¥20,000,000 (¥10,000,000 for prospective founders who haven't started yet), and it also offers a separate special provision for founders opening in vacant shopping-district storefronts, under which interest and credit guarantee fees are fully subsidized.
