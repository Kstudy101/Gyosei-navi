Beyond the nationwide childbirth lump-sum allowance (generally ¥500,000), several of Tokyo's 23 wards add their own benefits on top. But the structure and amount vary widely from ward to ward. This article compares six: Shibuya, Minato, Chiyoda, Setagaya, Shinagawa, and Shinjuku.

## Key points

- Wards that subsidize based on **actual delivery costs** (Minato, Chiyoda) can pay out the most, up to ¥310,000
- Shibuya pays a **flat ¥100,000 cap**, regardless of actual delivery costs
- Setagaya and Shinagawa combine the **national program (Pregnancy Support Payment) with their own fixed-amount payments**
- Shinjuku doesn't pay cash at all — it **subsidizes the cost of using** helpers and similar support services, which sets it apart fundamentally from the other five wards

## Four different program structures

The six wards compared here split into four broad patterns.

**① Tied to actual delivery costs (Minato, Chiyoda)**

These subsidize the gap between your actual delivery cost and the childbirth lump-sum allowance. The higher your delivery cost, the bigger the subsidy — up to a cap of ¥310,000 per birth. Conversely, if your delivery cost falls within the lump-sum allowance, the subsidy can be zero.

**② A flat amount regardless of actual cost (Shibuya)**

Whatever your delivery cost, you get a flat cap of ¥100,000 per birth (minus any supplementary benefit from your health insurance, if applicable). Since it doesn't scale with actual cost, the math is simple.

**③ National program plus a ward-specific flat payment (Setagaya, Shinagawa)**

Setagaya adds its own "Childbirth Cost Subsidy Program" (¥50,000 per child) on top of the national "Pregnancy Support Payment" (¥50,000 during pregnancy plus ¥50,000 per child after birth). Based on the official pages we checked, Shinagawa doesn't appear to have a ward-specific add-on.

**④ Subsidizing service costs instead of paying cash (Shinjuku)**

While the first three types all pay cash directly, Shinjuku's pre/postnatal support program subsidizes the fee when you use a childcare or housework helper, or a postpartum doula. If you don't use one of these support workers, there's no money to receive, and there's also a cap on eligible hours (for example, 40 hours from before birth through the child's first birthday, for a household with only one child). Unlike the "cash you receive" benefits in other wards, this is better described as "a service subsidy that pays off if you use it."
