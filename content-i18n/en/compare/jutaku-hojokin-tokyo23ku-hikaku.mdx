Every one of Tokyo's 23 wards offers its own home renovation subsidy, but the details differ significantly from ward to ward. This article compares six: Shibuya, Minato, Chiyoda, Setagaya, Shinagawa, and Shinjuku — organized not by "which has the biggest amount" but by "what kind of renovation each one covers."

## Key points

- The six wards' programs split into four broad types: **energy-saving renovation** (Chiyoda, Setagaya), **window-focused** (Minato), **combined** (Shinagawa), and **seismic** (Shibuya, Shinjuku)
- Subsidy calculations vary too — fixed rate, fixed amount, conditional — so a simple cap comparison can be misleading
- Even within the same "seismic" category, Shibuya and Shinjuku differ in subsidy rate and cap
- The practical approach is to **start from the type of renovation you want and find the matching ward's program**

## Why a simple amount comparison doesn't work

Unlike the 23 wards' childbirth benefits (compared in a separate article), which all address "the same event," home renovation subsidies differ by what they cover in the first place.

- Chiyoda and Setagaya cover **energy-saving renovation** (LED lighting, solar power, window insulation, and more)
- Minato focuses specifically on **windows** (high-insulation sashes)
- Shinagawa combines **eco renovation and barrier-free renovation**
- Shibuya and Shinjuku cover **seismic retrofit and demolition**, unrelated to energy efficiency

Lining these up by "which has the highest cap" compares dollar amounts for fundamentally different kinds of work, which can be misleading.

## Program type by ward

**① Energy-saving renovation type (Chiyoda, Setagaya)**

These cover a broad range of work that improves a home's energy efficiency — LED lighting, solar power, window insulation, and more. Chiyoda subsidizes 20-50% of eligible costs (up to ¥1,000,000), while Setagaya offers up to ¥200,000 combined across 4 categories. Note that Chiyoda's fiscal 2026 applications have closed after hitting the budget cap.

**② Window-focused type (Minato)**

This program focuses specifically on installing high-insulation sashes (windows). Residents get installation cost ÷ 4, up to ¥100,000, while condominium management associations can get up to ¥10,000,000 — a big difference in scale between individuals and associations.

**③ Combined type (Shinagawa)**

This is a broad program covering eco home renovation, barrier-free home renovation, and even some seismic work. It's 10% of the (pre-tax) construction cost, with residents capped at ¥200,000 and management associations or rental property owners capped at ¥1,000,000.

**④ Seismic type (Shibuya, Shinjuku)**

Unlike the energy-saving programs, these target earthquake preparedness. In Shibuya, you first get a free seismic diagnosis from the ward, and if your structural score comes back below 1.0, you can get subsidized for retrofit work (up to ¥1,500,000) or demolition costs (up to ¥1,000,000). Shinjuku subsidizes seismic retrofit work that raises the upper-structure score to 1.0 or above, covering three-quarters of eligible construction costs up to ¥3,000,000 — a higher cap than Shibuya's (though buildings that protrude onto the road or lack road access get a reduced amount).
